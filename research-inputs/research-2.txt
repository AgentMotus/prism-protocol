A Root System Framework for Human Behavioral Knowledge Graphs

Gerry Alvarez
Independent Researcher — Systems Psychology & Applied Mathematics
Mexico City, Mexico
Working Paper — v0.1 — February 2026

Abstract
We propose a mathematical framework for representing human conscious and unconscious
experience as vectors in a high-dimensional data space, and for analyzing the collective
structures that emerge when many such individual representations interact within a
distributed knowledge graph. Drawing on the theory of root systems in Lie algebras, spectral
graph theory, Lacanian structural psychoanalysis, Jungian depth psychology, and
Mesoamerican oneiric epistemology, we define a series of formal objects: the Behavioral
Data Vector Space, the Conscious-Unconscious Root Pair, the Behavioral Ledger Graph,
and the Social Ledger Symmetry Group. We conjecture that sufficiently rich collective
behavioral graphs exhibit symmetry properties that can be characterized by representations
of exceptional Lie groups, and that these symmetry properties correspond to the social and
planetary boundary conditions described by Doughnut Economics (Raworth, 2017). We
outline a decentralized implementation architecture in which each participant generates data
through a novel Proof of Work protocol—the mining of their own conscious and unconscious
experience—stored as self-sovereign identity tokens in distributed storage systems. This
framework provides a foundation for ethical AI governance, transparent social network
analysis, and a new mathematical psychology of collective consciousness.
Keywords: root systems, Lie algebras, knowledge graphs, collective consciousness, behavioral
data, spectral graph theory, Doughnut Economics, decentralized identity, psychoanalytic topology,
Mesoamerican epistemology, Proof of Work, E8 lattice

1. Introduction
Human experience unfolds across multiple scales simultaneously. At the individual level, each person
navigates a continuous interplay between conscious deliberation and unconscious processes—a
dynamic recognized by psychoanalytic traditions from Freud through Jung to Lacan, and mapped
phenomenologically by contemplative practices spanning millennia. At the social level, these
individual dynamics aggregate into collective patterns: shared cultural narratives, institutional

structures, economic systems, and what Jung (1959) termed the collective unconscious—recurring
archetypal structures that appear across unrelated populations. At the planetary level, these social
patterns interact with biophysical systems to produce the coupled social-ecological dynamics
described by the planetary boundaries framework (Rockstrom et al., 2009) and synthesized in the
Doughnut Economics model (Raworth, 2017).
Despite the evident interconnection of these scales, no formal mathematical framework exists to
represent them within a unified structure. Physics has pursued unification through gauge symmetry
and the exceptional Lie groups, culminating in proposals such as Lisi's (2007) E8 Theory of
Everything. Neuroscience has discovered that neural networks form cliques describable as simplices
in up to 11 dimensions (Reimann et al., 2017). Graph theory and network science have provided tools
for analyzing social structures. And the emerging field of geometric deep learning (Bronstein et al.,
2021) has shown that the symmetry properties of data—formalized through group theory and fiber
bundles—are fundamental to the design of effective neural network architectures.
This paper proposes to bridge these domains. We present a framework in which individual human
experience is represented as a pair of vectors (conscious and unconscious data streams) forming a
minimal root system, social interactions between individuals generate higher-rank root systems
through algebraic operations, and the collective knowledge graph of all participants has symmetry
properties that can be analyzed using the representation theory of Lie algebras. We ground this
framework in three intellectual traditions that have independently pursued the mapping of inner and
outer experience: the psychoanalytic tradition (particularly Lacan's topological psychoanalysis and
graph-theoretic models of the unconscious), the Jungian tradition of archetypal psychology and
collective consciousness, and Mesoamerican epistemology—particularly the Maya and Mexica
traditions that accorded the oneiric (dream) life equal epistemic status to waking experience.
We further propose a decentralized implementation architecture in which each participant contributes
their data through a novel Proof of Work protocol—the systematic recording and mining of their own
conscious and unconscious experience. This data, stored as self-sovereign identity tokens in
distributed systems, collectively forms a knowledge graph whose algebraic properties encode
information about the health of social and ecological systems. The framework is designed to serve as
a foundation for ethical AI governance: transparent in its collective analytics, private in its individual
data, and self-sovereign in its ownership structure.

2. Mathematical Preliminaries
We briefly review the mathematical structures required for our framework. Readers familiar with root
systems, spectral graph theory, and Lie algebras may proceed to Section 3.

2.1 Root Systems
Let E be a finite-dimensional real vector space equipped with a positive-definite inner product (·, ·). A
root system in E is a finite set ■ of nonzero vectors (called roots) satisfying the following axioms
(Humphreys, 1972):

(R1) ■ spans E and does not contain 0.
(R2) For each root ■ in ■, the only scalar multiples of ■ in ■ are ■ and −■.
(R3) For every pair of roots ■, ■ in ■, the integer 2(■, ■)/(■, ■) belongs to the integers
Z.
(R4) For each root ■ in ■, the reflection ■a defined by ■a(■) = ■ − 2(■, ■)/(■, ■) · ■
maps ■ to itself.
The rank of a root system is the dimension of E. The simplest root system, A1, consists of a single
vector and its negative in one dimension: {■, −■}. The reflection through the hyperplane
perpendicular to ■ simply maps ■ to −■ and vice versa. Higher-rank root systems (A2, B2, G2, and
the exceptional systems E6, E7, E8) arise when multiple roots interact through reflections in
higher-dimensional spaces.
A root system is indecomposable (or irreducible) when it cannot be partitioned into two mutually
orthogonal subsets. The classification theorem (Killing, 1888; Cartan, 1894) establishes that every
indecomposable root system belongs to one of the families An, Bn, Cn, Dn (the classical series) or one
of the exceptional systems G2, F4, E6, E7, E8. The E8 root system, with 240 roots in 8 dimensions, is
the largest exceptional root system and plays a distinguished role in theoretical physics as a candidate
symmetry group for unification theories.

2.2 Spectral Graph Theory
A graph G = (V, E) consists of a set of vertices V and a set of edges E. The adjacency matrix A of
G is the |V| x |V| matrix where Aij = 1 if vertices i and j are connected, and 0 otherwise (for weighted
graphs, Aij equals the edge weight). The graph Laplacian L = D − A, where D is the diagonal degree
matrix, encodes the combinatorial structure of the graph. The eigenvalues of L (the spectrum of the
graph) determine many global properties: connectivity, expansion, clustering structure, and symmetry
(Chung, 1997).
The automorphism group Aut(G) of a graph is the group of permutations of its vertices that preserve
the edge structure. This group encodes the symmetries of the graph. A central observation motivating
this paper is that the automorphism groups of certain highly structured graphs have representations
that decompose into irreducible representations corresponding to root systems of Lie algebras. If a
human behavioral knowledge graph exhibits such structure, its symmetry group carries information
about the collective organization of consciousness within the network.

2.3 Lacanian Graphs and Topological Psychoanalysis
Lacan (1966) introduced graph-theoretic and topological structures into psychoanalytic theory, most
notably the Graph of Desire (Seminar VI) and the Borromean knot (Seminar XXIII). In Lacan's
formalization, the subject is a node in a directed graph, the signifying chain is a directed path, and the
relationship between the subject and the Other is encoded in the graph's topology. Lacan's assertion
that “the unconscious is structured like a language” can be reformulated graph-theoretically: the
unconscious has a combinatorial structure that can be analyzed using the same algebraic tools applied
to formal languages and automata. This paper extends Lacan's intuition by proposing that the
unconscious not only has graph structure but that the symmetry group of this graph has specific

algebraic properties—namely, those of root systems.

3. The Behavioral Data Vector Space
We now introduce the core formal objects of our framework, beginning with the representation of
individual human experience as vectors in a well-defined data space.
Definition 1 (Behavioral Data Vector Space).
Let B = Rd be a d-dimensional real vector space equipped with the standard inner product,
where d is the number of measurable behavioral dimensions. We call B the Behavioral
Data Vector Space. Each dimension corresponds to a quantifiable aspect of human
experience: physiological measurements, self-reported psychological states, digital
interaction patterns, economic transactions, clinical records, social connections, temporal
activity patterns, and linguistic/semantic content of recorded experience. The value of d is
determined by the resolution of the measurement instruments and may increase as
measurement technology advances.
The critical property of B is that it is grounded in empirical data. Each dimension corresponds to a
measurable quantity, and each vector in B corresponds to a real data profile generated by a real
person over a defined time interval. The inner product (x, y) = x1y1 + x2y2 + ... + xdyd provides a
measure of similarity between two behavioral profiles, and the norm ||x|| = (x, x)1/2 provides a
measure of the intensity or magnitude of a behavioral profile.
Definition 2 (Conscious and Unconscious Data Streams).
For each individual i at time t, we define two complementary vectors in B:
The Conscious Data Vector ci(t) in Rd, whose components are populated by data the
individual deliberately generates or consents to record: journal entries, self-reports,
intentional digital activity (searches, messages, posts), clinical self-assessments, financial
transactions, and any other data stream the individual is aware of producing.
The Unconscious Data Vector ui(t) in Rd, whose components are populated by data
generated through processes the individual does not deliberately control: sleep and dream
patterns (recorded via journals or biometric devices), involuntary biometric signals (heart
rate variability, galvanic skin response, eye tracking), behavioral patterns inferred from
usage data that the individual is not consciously aware of, and—as recording technology
develops—reports from altered states of consciousness including dream content,
psychedelic experience reports, and meditation state descriptions.
The distinction between ci and ui is epistemological, not ontological: both vectors live in the same
space B, but they are populated through different modes of data generation. The conscious vector
reflects the individual's self-model; the unconscious vector reflects patterns that are real but not
self-represented. This mirrors the psychoanalytic distinction between the ego's self-narrative and the
unconscious processes that structure behavior without the subject's awareness.

Remark (Mesoamerican Epistemological Precedent). The Maya and Mexica civilizations
maintained systematic practices for recording and interpreting dream content (temictli) alongside
waking experience, according equal epistemic weight to both. Dream interpreters (tlamatini)
functioned as specialists in the analysis of the unconscious data stream, using culturally transmitted
frameworks for classification and cross-referencing. The present framework can be understood as a
digital reconstruction of this epistemological commitment: the oneiric life as a data source of equal
validity to the waking life, recorded with equivalent rigor and analyzed within a single mathematical
structure. Much of this knowledge was destroyed during European colonization; this framework is
offered in part as a contribution to its recovery through contemporary means.

4. The Conscious-Unconscious Root Pair
Definition 3 (The A1HM Root System).
For each individual i, define the integrated behavioral vector ai = ci − ui (the difference
between the conscious and unconscious data vectors, representing the degree and
direction of asymmetry between what the individual knows about themselves and what
operates below awareness). The A1 Human Mind root system (A1HM) for individual i
is the set:
■i = {ai, −ai}
This is a rank-1 root system in the one-dimensional subspace of B spanned by ai. The
reflection ■a maps ai to −ai and vice versa.
The A1HM root system captures a fundamental psychodynamic structure: the individual's conscious
self-model (ci) and their unconscious behavioral pattern (ui) define a single axis of variation, and the
reflection between them represents the psychoanalytic process of making the unconscious
conscious—or equivalently, the projection of unconscious material onto external reality. In Lacanian
terms, the reflection corresponds to the mirror stage: the subject's identification with an image that is
both self and not-self, located on the perpendicular plane between the conscious and unconscious
positions.
The inner product (ai, ai) = ||ci − ui||2 measures the degree of dissociation between an individual's
conscious self-model and their unconscious patterns. A small norm indicates high self-awareness (the
conscious and unconscious data streams are closely aligned). A large norm indicates significant
unconscious material that has not been integrated into the self-model. This provides a formal,
measurable analogue to the psychoanalytic concept of repression.

5. From Individual to Social: The Behavioral Ledger Graph
Definition 4 (Behavioral Ledger).
A Behavioral Ledger for individual i is the time-indexed collection of their data vectors:
BLi = {(ci(t), ui(t)) : t in T}, where T is the set of time intervals at which data is recorded.

The Behavioral Ledger is stored as a self-sovereign digital identity token (a Human
Non-Fungible Token, or HNFT) in a distributed storage system, controlled by the
individual's private key. It is the individual's contribution to the network and constitutes
their Proof of Work: the mining of their own experience.
Definition 5 (Behavioral Ledger Graph).
The Behavioral Ledger Graph G = (V, E, w) is a weighted graph where:
• V is the set of all participants, each associated with a Behavioral Ledger BLi.
• E is the set of edges representing interactions between participants (therapy sessions,
economic transactions, community membership, shared DLN participation,
communication events).
• w: E → R+ is a weight function encoding the strength/frequency of interaction.
Each vertex carries an attribute vector ai (the integrated behavioral vector from Definition
3). The graph is dynamic: both vertices and edges evolve over time as participants join,
interact, and update their Behavioral Ledgers.
Definition 6 (Social Ledger).
A Social Ledger is a connected subgraph S of G whose vertices share specified attributes
(geographical location, cultural context, clinical condition, shared goals, ideological
alignment, or other grouping criteria). Social Ledgers aggregate individual Behavioral
Ledgers into coherent communities. When a Social Ledger acquires governance structure
and aligns with a defined social or planetary objective, it becomes a Distributed Living
Network (DLN).

6. Symmetry Analysis of the Collective Graph
The central mathematical question of this framework is: what are the symmetry properties of the
Behavioral Ledger Graph, and do these symmetries have Lie-algebraic structure?
Definition 7 (Social Symmetry Group).
The Social Symmetry Group of the Behavioral Ledger Graph G is the automorphism
group Aut(G)—the group of permutations of V that preserve the edge structure and the
vertex attribute vectors (up to a specified tolerance). Formally, ■ in Aut(G) if and only if
for all edges (i,j) in E, (■(i), ■(j)) is also in E with the same weight, and ||a■(i) − ai|| is
within tolerance ■.
The automorphism group encodes which participants in the network are structurally
interchangeable—which positions in the social graph are symmetric. A rich symmetry group
indicates a highly organized collective structure. A trivial symmetry group indicates a disordered or
individualistic network.

Conjecture 1 (Root System Emergence).
For sufficiently large and diverse Behavioral Ledger Graphs with balanced
representation of conscious and unconscious data streams, the irreducible
representations of the approximate Social Symmetry Group decompose into components
whose weight diagrams correspond to root systems of exceptional Lie algebras (E6, E7, or
E8). The rank of the emergent root system increases with the number of independent
social and planetary boundary variables that are actively measured and addressed by the
network's Distributed Living Networks.
This conjecture is empirically testable. Given a sufficiently large dataset of behavioral ledgers and
their interaction graph, one can compute the approximate automorphism group, decompose its
representations, and compare the resulting weight diagrams against the known classification of root
systems. The conjecture predicts that the structure will converge toward exceptional types rather than
classical types, reflecting the indecomposable nature of social-planetary boundary systems as argued
by Raworth (2017).

7. Connection to Doughnut Economics
Raworth's (2017) Doughnut Economics model identifies 12 social foundation boundaries (health,
education, income, political voice, social equity, gender equality, housing, networks, energy, water,
food, jobs) nested within 9 planetary ceiling boundaries (climate change, ocean acidification,
chemical pollution, nitrogen/phosphorus loading, freshwater withdrawals, land conversion,
biodiversity loss, air pollution, ozone layer depletion). Humanity's challenge is to meet the social
foundations without overshooting the planetary ceilings—to live within the “safe and just space” of
the doughnut.
Definition 8 (Boundary Indicator Projection).
Define a Boundary Indicator Function ■: G → R21 that maps the Behavioral Ledger
Graph to a 21-dimensional vector (12 social + 9 planetary indicators), where each
component aggregates relevant data from the graph's vertices and edges. The image of ■
at any time t locates the network's collective state within or outside the doughnut's safe
zone. The spectral properties of the graph Laplacian restricted to each boundary
dimension provide early warning signals for boundary transgression.
The 21 boundary dimensions are not independent—social foundations and planetary ceilings interact
in complex ways. This interaction structure is precisely what the root system framework is designed
to capture. The conjecture that the emergent symmetry group has exceptional Lie algebra structure
reflects the empirical observation that social and planetary boundaries form an indecomposable
system: one cannot address climate change without addressing energy access, which connects to
income, which connects to health, and so on. The E8 lattice, as the largest exceptional root system,
represents the most complex indecomposable symmetry—a mathematical object whose structure
mirrors the irreducible interconnection of civilization-scale challenges.

8. Decentralized Implementation Architecture
The framework requires an implementation architecture that is simultaneously transparent in its
collective analytics, private in its individual data, and self-sovereign in its ownership structure. We
outline the key components.

8.1 Proof of Work as Experience Mining
In conventional blockchain protocols, Proof of Work (PoW) requires computational expenditure to
secure the network—a process widely criticized for its energy waste. We propose a reconceptualized
PoW in which the “work” is the generation of genuine human behavioral data. Each participant who
records their conscious experience (journal entries, clinical self-reports, intentional activity) and/or
their unconscious data (dream journals, biometric streams, altered state reports) contributes a block of
authenticated data to the network. This contribution simultaneously enriches the collective
knowledge graph, strengthens the network's analytical power (more data produces more robust
symmetry analysis), and earns the participant access to the network's services and governance.

8.2 Self-Sovereign Identity and Distributed Storage
Each participant's Behavioral Ledger is stored as a Human Non-Fungible Token (HNFT) in a
distributed and decentralized storage system (such as Filecoin, Walrus, or equivalent). The HNFT is
controlled by the participant's private key via a Decentralized Identifier (DID) container. Data is
encrypted at rest and in transit. Access is granted selectively—a therapist can access clinical history
with the patient's cryptographic consent, but aggregate network analytics operate on
privacy-preserving representations (zero-knowledge proofs, homomorphic encryption, or differential
privacy mechanisms) that reveal collective patterns without exposing individual data.

8.3 Ethical AI Governance
The framework is designed as an architecture for ethical artificial intelligence. AI systems trained on
the Behavioral Ledger Graph operate under three constraints: transparency (the graph's symmetry
properties and boundary indicator projections are publicly auditable), privacy (individual data
remains encrypted and self-sovereign; AI accesses only privacy-preserving aggregates), and
accountability (governance decisions derived from graph analytics are executed through smart
contracts whose logic is open-source and verifiable). This architecture ensures that AI serves as a tool
for collective self-understanding rather than a mechanism for surveillance or manipulation by
centralized authorities.

9. AI Architecture: Symmetry-Aware Intelligence
The E8 framework is not merely a governance layer for artificial intelligence—it is a data
architecture that makes AI systems technically superior by organizing information along the natural
symmetry axes of human experience. The core principle derives from geometric deep learning
(Bronstein et al., 2021): neural networks designed to respect the symmetry group of their input data
(equivariant networks) consistently outperform generic architectures in sample efficiency,
generalization, and computational cost. If human behavioral data exhibits root-system symmetry as
conjectured, then AI systems built on this structure inherit these advantages automatically. This
section details five concrete mechanisms.

9.1 Symmetry-Structured Retrieval-Augmented Generation
Current Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) systems store documents as vectors in a flat
embedding space and retrieve candidates by cosine similarity, treating all dimensions equally. This
results in O(n) search complexity over n documents even with approximate nearest neighbor
indexing. We propose a Root-Structured RAG architecture that exploits the symmetry properties of
the Behavioral Ledger Graph.
Definition 9 (Weyl Chamber Indexing).
Given a root system ■ in the Behavioral Data Vector Space B, the Weyl chambers are
the connected components of B after removing all reflection hyperplanes. Each Weyl
chamber is a convex cone that partitions the data space into regions of equivalent
symmetry type. A Weyl Chamber Index assigns each behavioral data vector to its
containing chamber, creating a hierarchical addressing scheme: first identify the chamber
(coarse symmetry class), then search within the chamber (fine-grained similarity). For an
E8 root system, there are |W(E8)| = 696,729,600 Weyl chambers, providing an extremely
fine-grained natural partition of the data space.
The practical consequence is that RAG queries first identify the relevant Weyl chamber—a
constant-time operation based on the query vector's position relative to root hyperplanes—then
restrict nearest-neighbor search to that chamber and its algebraically adjacent chambers. This reduces
the effective search space by orders of magnitude while guaranteeing that retrieved context respects
the symmetry structure of the behavioral domain. For clinical applications, an AI copilot assisting a
therapist retrieves relevant case patterns by navigating the algebraic structure of the clinical
knowledge graph: the root reflection between a conscious symptom presentation and its unconscious
origin becomes a computable retrieval operation, not a heuristic similarity search.

9.2 E8 Lattice Vector Quantization and Storage
The E8 lattice achieves the densest sphere packing in 8 dimensions (Viazovska, 2017), a result for
which the Fields Medal was awarded. This mathematical fact has a direct engineering consequence:
vector quantization using the E8 lattice produces the most space-efficient encoding of
8-dimensional data that is physically possible.
Definition 10 (E8 Lattice Vector Database).

An E8 Lattice Vector Database stores behavioral data vectors by projecting them onto
the E8 lattice and indexing them by their nearest lattice point. The Voronoi cell of each
lattice point serves as a quantization bucket. Because the E8 lattice has the highest packing
density and kissing number (240) in 8 dimensions, this indexing scheme achieves: (a)
optimal storage efficiency—the most data points per unit of index space; (b) optimal
nearest-neighbor query performance—each lattice point has exactly 240 neighbors at
minimum distance, providing a fixed and computationally efficient neighbor structure;
and (c) natural multi-scale resolution—coarser queries use larger shells of the lattice, finer
queries use the Voronoi cell structure.
Current vector databases (Pinecone, Weaviate, Qdrant, Milvus) use approximate nearest neighbor
algorithms (HNSW, IVF-PQ) that impose no algebraic structure on the index. An E8-structured index
exploits lattice geometry to provide exact nearest-neighbor guarantees within each Voronoi cell while
maintaining logarithmic search complexity across cells. For the decentralized implementation, each
node's HNFT data is quantized to its nearest E8 lattice point, enabling efficient distributed queries
across the network without requiring any single node to hold the complete index.

9.3 Equivariant Attention and Context Optimization
The Transformer architecture computes attention between all pairs of tokens in a sequence at O(n2)
cost—the primary bottleneck for large context windows. If the data has known symmetry properties,
attention can be restricted to symmetry-related positions, dramatically reducing the effective context
size.
Definition 11 (Root-Equivariant Attention).
A Root-Equivariant Attention mechanism constrains the attention matrix Aij to be
non-zero only when the behavioral vectors of tokens i and j are related by a root system
operation (reflection, Weyl group rotation, or root-direction translation). Formally, Aij = 0
unless there exists a Weyl group element ■ such that ||aj − ■(ai)|| is within tolerance. This
reduces effective attention from O(n2) to O(n · |orbit|), where |orbit| is determined by the
symmetry structure rather than the sequence length.
Two behavioral profiles related by a root reflection (e.g., a conscious symptom pattern and its
unconscious complement) carry maximal mutual information and should attend strongly. Two
profiles in orthogonal Weyl chambers carry minimal mutual information and need not attend at all.
The root system provides exactly this relevance map, replacing learned attention weights with
algebraically derived constraints that encode domain knowledge about the structure of human
experience. This is not a heuristic approximation—it is a mathematically principled sparsification that
preserves all symmetry-relevant information while discarding noise.

9.4 Root-Decomposed Federated Learning
In a decentralized network where each participant controls their own data, training AI models
requires aggregating information across nodes without centralizing the data. Standard federated
learning (McMahan et al., 2017) averages gradients across participants, treating all gradient

directions equally. This converges slowly when data distributions are heterogeneous—as they
invariably are in a global behavioral network.
Definition 12 (Root-Decomposed Federated Learning).
In Root-Decomposed Federated Learning, each Distributed Living Network computes
model gradients projected onto its root subspace—the subspace of B spanned by the roots
associated with that DLN's boundary function. The global model update combines these
projections using the Cartan matrix of the root system, which encodes interaction angles
between roots. Orthogonal root contributions sum directly; non-orthogonal contributions
are combined according to the algebraic inner product structure. Each DLN transmits only
its
low-dimensional
root-subspace
projection—a
communication-efficient,
privacy-preserving, algebraically principled protocol that outperforms naive gradient
averaging.
This approach is communication-efficient (each DLN transmits a projection of dimension equal to the
rank of its root subsystem, not the full model gradient), algebraically principled (the combination
rule is derived from the Cartan matrix rather than ad hoc averaging), and privacy-preserving by
construction (individual data never leaves the participant's device; only the DLN-level aggregate
root-subspace gradient is shared, and this aggregate discards individual-level variation orthogonal to
the root direction).

9.5 Implications: Resolving the Clinical AI Trilemma
Current mental health AI faces a trilemma: clinical utility requires rich personal data, privacy requires
minimal data exposure, and generalization requires learning from many patients. The E8 framework
resolves this trilemma by providing a mathematically principled way to learn collective patterns (via
the symmetry group of the graph) without accessing individual data (via root-decomposed federated
learning and privacy-preserving projections).
An AI copilot for therapists trained within this architecture would access the algebraic structure of
the collective clinical knowledge graph—the patterns, symmetries, and root reflections between
conscious presentation and unconscious dynamics—without ever seeing any individual patient's
records. This is not a compromise between utility and privacy; it is a superior representation of
clinical knowledge, because the symmetry structure is precisely the level at which clinical patterns
generalize across individuals. The root reflection between a conscious symptom and its unconscious
origin is a universal clinical structure; the specific content of any one patient's symptom is private.
The framework separates these levels by mathematical construction, enabling AI systems that are
simultaneously more capable and more ethical than current approaches.
More broadly, this architecture provides a template for ethical AI across domains: the E8 framework
ensures that AI systems learn structure (symmetries, patterns, root reflections) rather than content
(individual data points), achieving a mathematical separation between collective intelligence and
individual privacy. This separation is not imposed by policy but emerges from the algebra itself—a
system that is private and transparent not by regulation but by design.

10. Open Conjectures and Research Program
We conclude by stating the principal open questions that define the research program initiated by this
paper.
Conjecture 2 (Integrality Approximation).
When behavioral data vectors are appropriately quantized (discretized into categorical or
ordinal scales aligned with clinically validated instruments), the inner product ratios
between root pairs approximate integers with error bounded by a function of the
quantization resolution. As resolution increases, the approximation improves, and the
quantized system converges to a formal root system in the algebraic sense.
Conjecture 3 (Collective Unconscious as Graph Property).
In sufficiently large Behavioral Ledger Graphs where unconscious data streams (dream
journals, altered state reports, involuntary biometric patterns) are recorded with fidelity
comparable to conscious data streams, the unconscious data vectors of unconnected
participants exhibit statistically significant correlations that cannot be explained by
shared environment or communication. These correlations manifest as non-trivial
structure in the graph Laplacian's null space, providing an empirical signature of a
phenomenon consistent with Jung's collective unconscious—a shared pattern space
accessible through individual unconscious processes.
Conjecture 4 (Lacanian Topological Types).
The local topology of the Behavioral Ledger Graph around individual vertices (the
structure of each person's ego neighborhood in the graph) can be classified into types that
correspond to Lacanian structural categories. Specifically, the homology groups of the
local subgraph distinguish neurotic, psychotic, and perverse structures as different
topological types, formalizing Lacan's clinical topology in graph-theoretic terms.
Conjecture 5 (Boundary Transgression Early Warning).
Changes in the spectral gap of the Behavioral Ledger Graph's Laplacian, restricted to
specific boundary indicator dimensions, provide leading indicators of social or planetary
boundary transgression. A narrowing spectral gap in a given dimension signals that the
network is losing resilience in that domain—approaching the edge of the doughnut's safe
zone—before the transgression is observable in traditional aggregate indicators.

11. Conclusion
We have presented a formal framework for representing human consciousness at multiple scales
using algebraic structures from Lie theory and spectral graph theory. The framework defines
individual experience as a root pair in a behavioral data vector space, social interaction as the
generation of higher-rank root systems through graph construction, and collective consciousness as

an emergent symmetry property of the resulting knowledge graph. It connects psychoanalytic theory
(Lacan, Jung) with mathematical physics (Lie algebras, root systems), ecological economics
(Doughnut Economics), indigenous epistemology (Mesoamerican dream science), and decentralized
technology (blockchain, self-sovereign identity, distributed storage).
The framework is offered not as a completed theory but as a research program. Its conjectures are
empirically testable, its definitions are formally precise, and its implementation architecture is
technologically feasible with current infrastructure. We invite collaboration from mathematicians,
psychologists, physicists, computer scientists, indigenous knowledge holders, and anyone committed
to the proposition that understanding consciousness—individual and collective—is both a scientific
challenge and a moral imperative.
